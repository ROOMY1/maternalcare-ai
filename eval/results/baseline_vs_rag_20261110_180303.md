**q03 — Which medicine is recommended to prevent eclampsia in women with severe pre-eclampsia?** (gold: WHO_preeclampsia-eclampsia_2011.pdf p.10)

*Baseline (no retrieval):* In women with severe pre-eclampsia, magnesium sulfate is the recommended medication to prevent eclampsia. Magnesium sulfate is a critical treatment for the prevention and management of pre-eclampsia and eclampsia. It works by relaxing the muscles in the walls of blood vessels, which can help lower blood pressure and reduce the risk of seizures.

It's important to note that magnesium sulfate should be administered under strict medical supervision due to its potential side effects, including respiratory depression, cardiac arrest, and toxicity. The treatment should be closely monitored, and the patient's serum magnesium levels should be checked regularly.

In addition to magnesium sulfate, other supportive measures and treatments may be necessary, depending on the severity of the condition and the overall health of the patient. These can include close monitoring of maternal and fetal status, blood pressure control, and in some cases, early delivery if the condition is severe and poses a risk to the mother or the baby.

**q08 — Can I take NSAIDs such as ibuprofen after 20 weeks of pregnancy?** (gold: FDA_nsaids-20-weeks_2020.pdf p.1)

*Baseline (no retrieval):* It is generally not recommended to take NSAIDs like ibuprofen during the second and third trimesters of pregnancy, including after 20 weeks, without consulting your healthcare provider first. NSAIDs can potentially cause complications, including reduced fetal growth and an increased risk of bleeding for the mother. 

However, if you are experiencing pain or inflammation, it's important to discuss your symptoms and any medication needs with your healthcare provider. They can provide guidance on safe pain management options that are appropriate for your specific situation and stage of pregnancy. They may recommend other pain relief methods or prescribe a safer alternative if necessary.

*RAG:* Ibuprofen is usually not recommended after week 20 of pregnancy. Some reports suggest that using NSAIDs in the third trimester can increase the chance of preterm delivery or low birth weight. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recommends not using NSAIDs after week 20 of pregnancy unless your healthcare provider specifically recommends it. If a healthcare provider decides NSAIDs are needed after week 20 of pregnancy, they should be used at the lowest dose for the shortest time. [1][3]

**q16 — Which medicine is recommended to prevent postpartum haemorrhage?** (gold: WHO_pph-prevention-treatment_2012.pdf p.10)

*Baseline (no retrieval):* To prevent postpartum hemorrhage, oxytocics are commonly recommended. One of the most widely used medications is oxytocin, which is administered intravenously or intramuscularly after delivery to help contract the uterus and reduce bleeding. Other oxytocin-like medications, such as methylergonovine, can also be used, but they are less commonly used due to potential side effects.

It's important to note that the specific medication and its administration should be determined by a healthcare provider based on the individual patient's condition and medical history. Proper management of postpartum hemorrhage also includes close monitoring and other interventions as necessary.
